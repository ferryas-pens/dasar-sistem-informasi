Quality asks whether the information is good. Value asks whether anything changes because of it.

5.1

Information has value and it has a cost

Definition

Value=(decision outcome with the information−decision outcome without it)−cost of obtaining it

The hidden condition: if no decision depends on the information, both outcomes are the same and the difference is zero, however good the information is.

For the organisation that produces it, every piece of information demands four costs:

Recorder's timeAlways taken from time spent on something else.
StoragePlus the upkeep that keeps it readable for years.
Reader's attentionThe most often forgotten. Storage gets cheaper; attention does not grow.
TrustShrinks every time someone finds a wrong figure.

5.4

More data does not always mean more value

The first and biggest reason: attention does not grow. Move the number of reports and watch a fixed amount of attention being split.

Illustration: the fixed bar width stands for one day's attention. Equal shares are a simplification.

Attention does not growAdding one report can make ten other reports less useful.
Extra data delays the decisionAsking for more data is often a polite way to postpone. Sometimes what is missing is not data but courage.
Unused data quietly decaysA column nobody reads is a column nobody checks.

Behind the scenes · the life of an unused report

The first monthsThe author still checks it now and then; errors are found and fixed.
Months without a questionChecking stops. The report keeps coming out out of habit.
Nobody reads itNot even its author. Errors pile up.
Years laterIts figures are used for a real decision: neat, and long since wrong.

A test before adding anything: if this number changed, which decision would change with it? In Chapter 8 this test is called the so-what test.

5.5

Value is not always money

Something that cannot be measured does not have zero value. Do not invent numbers. Write it down as a non-numeric consideration and leave the weighing to the people who bear the consequences.

Money

Losses from ordering the wrong ingredientsCan be calculated fairly precisely

Time

Working hours lost looking for filesCan be calculated, then converted to money if needed

Safety

A dosage error in a health serviceMust not simply be converted to money

Trust

Customers who leave without complainingHard to measure, but must not be ignored
